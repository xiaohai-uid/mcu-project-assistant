# Define the paying customer and paid promise

Type: grilling
Status: resolved
Blocked by: None

## Resolution

- First paying customer: independent hardware and IoT developers who need to turn a device idea into a buildable, wireable, uploadable prototype without repeatedly resolving board and component facts by hand.
- Paid promise: provide a reproducible path from a user's project input to a compilable firmware artifact, explicit wiring constraints, and clearly labeled evidence showing what was proposed, compiled, uploaded, simulated, or physically observed.
- Student users are an acquisition and learning tier with free or low-cost limits; they are not the first commercial operating target.
- Small hardware teams are a later expansion of the same core product, not a separate engine.
- Local-first is valuable because the user's source code, board choices, build artifacts, and evidence can stay on their machine while the product remains useful without a hosted queue.

## Question

Who is the first paying customer, what painful MCU workflow are they buying relief from, and what single outcome must the first paid release promise? Compare beginner maker, engineering freelancer, education lab, small hardware team, and prototype service provider. The answer must include the input they bring, the artifact/evidence they receive, and why local-first is valuable enough to pay for.
